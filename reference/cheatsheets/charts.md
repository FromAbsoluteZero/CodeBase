# Charts

Chapter 12 teaches these; Chapter 42 applies them to writing for an audience.

| Question | Chart | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution of one variable | Histogram · boxplot for comparing groups | 12 |
| Relationship between two | Scatter plot, with a fitted line only if a line is defensible | 12 |
| Change over time | Line chart, time on the x-axis | 12, 28 |
| Comparison across categories | Bar chart, sorted by value unless the order carries meaning | 12 |
| Many small comparisons | Small multiples beat one crowded chart | 12 |
| Part of a whole | A sorted bar chart, not a pie; a stacked bar only for two or three parts | 12 |

| Rule | Why | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Start the y-axis at zero | For bars, always. For lines, only if truncation would mislead | 12 |
| Avoid dual axes | Two scales invite an invented relationship | 12 |
| Colour | Sequential for ordered values, diverging around a meaningful midpoint. Never red against green alone | 12 |
| Labels | Label the series directly rather than making the reader use a legend | 12 |
| Chartjunk | Remove anything that does not carry information | 12 |
| Title | State the finding, not the variables: "Returns rose after the March price change", not "Returns by month" | 42 |
