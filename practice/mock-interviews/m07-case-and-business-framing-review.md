# M07 · Case and business framing: review sheet

Read this only after you have sat the [mock interview](m07-case-and-business-framing.md).

## 1. Q168

A stakeholder wants to add AI to a workflow. How do you evaluate whether it is worth doing?

<sub>Chapters [1](../by-chapter/ch01.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Define the decision being made, the cost of each error, the baseline, and the data available. Ask what changes operationally if the model works, and whether a rule or a report would suffice.

## 2. Q169

Your model is accurate but adoption is low. What do you investigate?

<sub>Chapters [42](../by-chapter/ch42.md) · [43](../by-chapter/ch43.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Trust and explainability, workflow fit, latency, whether the output arrives at the moment of decision, and whether the metric optimized matches what users are accountable for.

## 3. Q170

How would you measure the business value of a deployed model?

<sub>Chapters [15](../by-chapter/ch15.md) · [43](../by-chapter/ch43.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Tie predictions to decisions and decisions to outcomes, ideally with a holdout or staged rollout. Estimate value as the change in the outcome metric against the baseline, net of serving and maintenance cost.

## 4. Q174

Revenue is down 8%. How do you investigate?

<sub>Chapters [1](../by-chapter/ch01.md) · [12](../by-chapter/ch12.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Decompose before theorizing: price against volume, new against returning, by segment, region, and channel, and check whether the comparison period is fair. Strong answers rule out measurement causes — a tracking change, a pipeline failure, a calendar effect — before reaching for commercial explanations.

## 5. Q176

How do you handle a request for a number by end of day when the data is messy?

<sub>Chapters [42](../by-chapter/ch42.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Deliver a number with its caveats attached rather than nothing, and be explicit about which direction the uncertainty runs. Strong answers scope down to what can be answered defensibly in the time, and say plainly what the fuller answer would require.

## 6. Q178

What would make you refuse to run an analysis?

<sub>Chapters [44](../by-chapter/ch44.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** When the design guarantees a predetermined conclusion, when the data was obtained without a basis for its intended use, or when the output would harm people the analysis cannot answer for. Strong answers distinguish refusing the framing from refusing the question, and offer the version they would run.

## 7. Q183

You are given a vague request with no clear success metric. What do you do?

<sub>Chapters [1](../by-chapter/ch01.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Work backward from the decision the output will inform, propose a measurable proxy, agree it in writing, and start with the smallest version that tests the assumption.
