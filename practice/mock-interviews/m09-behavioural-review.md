# M09 · Behavioural conversation: review sheet

Read this only after you have sat the [mock interview](m09-behavioural.md).

## 1. Q179

Tell me about a model or analysis that failed. What happened?

<sub>No chapter: about how you work</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Pick a real failure, state the impact plainly, explain the root cause technically, and describe what you changed in your process afterward. Avoid failures whose cause was entirely someone else.

## 2. Q180

Describe a time you disagreed with a stakeholder about an approach.

<sub>No chapter: about how you work</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Show that you understood their reasoning, brought evidence rather than assertion, and describe the resolution including cases where you changed your own position.

## 3. Q181

How do you decide when a model is good enough to ship?

<sub>Chapters [23](../by-chapter/ch23.md) · [43](../by-chapter/ch43.md) · [44](../by-chapter/ch44.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Against a pre-agreed threshold tied to the decision cost, with monitoring and rollback in place. Strong answers mention agreeing the bar before building, not after seeing results.

## 4. Q182

How do you keep current in a field that changes this quickly?

<sub>Chapters [45](../by-chapter/ch45.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Name specific concrete sources and a habit rather than a list of newsletters. Mention how you decide what to ignore, which is the harder and more revealing skill.

## 5. Q183

You are given a vague request with no clear success metric. What do you do?

<sub>Chapters [1](../by-chapter/ch01.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Work backward from the decision the output will inform, propose a measurable proxy, agree it in writing, and start with the smallest version that tests the assumption.

## 6. Q184

How do you prioritize when three people want three things?

<sub>No chapter: about how you work</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** Make the trade visible rather than resolving it privately: state what each request costs and what it displaces, and get the requesters into the same decision. Strong answers describe a default ordering — decisions with a deadline over exploratory work — and communicate the sequence rather than going quiet.

## 7. Q185

Why analytics?

<sub>Chapters [1](../by-chapter/ch01.md)</sub>

**What a strong answer contains.** No correct answer, and the interviewer is listening for something specific rather than a general enthusiasm for data. Strong answers name a concrete thing the candidate found out, why it mattered, and what they did next.
