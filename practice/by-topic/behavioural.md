# Behavioural and working style

Not a formality. Specific stories beat general claims every time.

Track: Business and behavioural · 7 questions · [All topics](README.md) · [Practice home](../README.md)

Levels: any 7. Types: behavioural 7.

Answer each question aloud before you open its note.

<a id="q179"></a>

**Q179.** Tell me about a model or analysis that failed. What happened?

<sub>No chapter: about how you work &nbsp;·&nbsp; any level &nbsp;·&nbsp; behavioural</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Pick a real failure, state the impact plainly, explain the root cause technically, and describe what you changed in your process afterward. Avoid failures whose cause was entirely someone else.

</details>

<a id="q180"></a>

**Q180.** Describe a time you disagreed with a stakeholder about an approach.

<sub>No chapter: about how you work &nbsp;·&nbsp; any level &nbsp;·&nbsp; behavioural</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Show that you understood their reasoning, brought evidence rather than assertion, and describe the resolution including cases where you changed your own position.

</details>

<a id="q181"></a>

**Q181.** How do you decide when a model is good enough to ship?

<sub>Chapters [23](../by-chapter/ch23.md) · [43](../by-chapter/ch43.md) · [44](../by-chapter/ch44.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; any level &nbsp;·&nbsp; behavioural</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Against a pre-agreed threshold tied to the decision cost, with monitoring and rollback in place. Strong answers mention agreeing the bar before building, not after seeing results.

</details>

<a id="q182"></a>

**Q182.** How do you keep current in a field that changes this quickly?

<sub>Chapters [45](../by-chapter/ch45.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; any level &nbsp;·&nbsp; behavioural</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Name specific concrete sources and a habit rather than a list of newsletters. Mention how you decide what to ignore, which is the harder and more revealing skill.

</details>

<a id="q183"></a>

**Q183.** You are given a vague request with no clear success metric. What do you do?

<sub>Chapters [1](../by-chapter/ch01.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; any level &nbsp;·&nbsp; behavioural</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Work backward from the decision the output will inform, propose a measurable proxy, agree it in writing, and start with the smallest version that tests the assumption.

</details>

<a id="q184"></a>

**Q184.** How do you prioritize when three people want three things?

<sub>No chapter: about how you work &nbsp;·&nbsp; any level &nbsp;·&nbsp; behavioural</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Make the trade visible rather than resolving it privately: state what each request costs and what it displaces, and get the requesters into the same decision. Strong answers describe a default ordering — decisions with a deadline over exploratory work — and communicate the sequence rather than going quiet.

</details>

<a id="q185"></a>

**Q185.** Why analytics?

<sub>Chapters [1](../by-chapter/ch01.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; any level &nbsp;·&nbsp; behavioural</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

No correct answer, and the interviewer is listening for something specific rather than a general enthusiasm for data. Strong answers name a concrete thing the candidate found out, why it mattered, and what they did next.

</details>
