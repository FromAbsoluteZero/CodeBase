## Questions that start here (5)

<a id="q169"></a>

**Q169.** Your model is accurate but adoption is low. What do you investigate?

<sub>Chapters [42](../by-chapter/ch42.md) · [43](../by-chapter/ch43.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; intermediate &nbsp;·&nbsp; scenario</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Trust and explainability, workflow fit, latency, whether the output arrives at the moment of decision, and whether the metric optimized matches what users are accountable for.

</details>

<a id="q172"></a>

**Q172.** How do you explain model uncertainty to a non-technical audience?

<sub>Chapters [42](../by-chapter/ch42.md) · [8](../by-chapter/ch08.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; intermediate &nbsp;·&nbsp; scenario</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Use frequencies rather than probabilities, give a concrete error rate in cases per hundred, state what the model does not know, and name the decision the number should and should not drive.

</details>

<a id="q173"></a>

**Q173.** A stakeholder wants a dashboard showing everything. What do you ask?

<sub>Chapters [42](../by-chapter/ch42.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; beginner &nbsp;·&nbsp; scenario</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

What decision it supports, who makes it, and how often. Strong answers explain that a dashboard showing everything gets read by nobody, and offer to build the three numbers that would change an action, with the rest available on request.

</details>

<a id="q175"></a>

**Q175.** Your analysis contradicts what a senior stakeholder believes. What do you do?

<sub>Chapters [42](../by-chapter/ch42.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; intermediate &nbsp;·&nbsp; scenario</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Check your own work first, hard, including the data lineage. Then present the finding privately with the method visible, invite the specific objection, and state what evidence would change your mind. Strong answers treat being wrong as a live possibility without abandoning the finding if it survives scrutiny.

</details>

<a id="q176"></a>

**Q176.** How do you handle a request for a number by end of day when the data is messy?

<sub>Chapters [42](../by-chapter/ch42.md) &nbsp;·&nbsp; intermediate &nbsp;·&nbsp; scenario</sub>

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer contains</summary>

Deliver a number with its caveats attached rather than nothing, and be explicit about which direction the uncertainty runs. Strong answers scope down to what can be answered defensibly in the time, and say plainly what the fuller answer would require.

</details>
